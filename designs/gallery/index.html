Production specs

    

      
Back print
DTF. Send dtf/<design>-<light|dark>.svg: flat vector paths, 2 solid inks, no effects or semi-transparency, sized 14 × 21 in. A 4200 × 6300 px PNG is also in print/. Artwork fills about 12 in wide. Place ~3 in below the back collar.
DTF clean-up
No grain speckle, every splatter dot at least ~1 mm, and anything under 0.6 mm removed in tracing, so nothing tiny peels off the film.
Light vs dark
Every design has a -light file (black ink, for white/beige/lavender) and a -dark file (off-white ink, for black/navy/green/chocolate). Red stays the same: close to Pantone 485 C.
Front embroidery
Left chest, 2.2 in wide, 3.3–4.3 in tall. Send the *-embroidery-*.svg (all text is already outlined) to your digitizer for DST/PES. Expect roughly 6–10k stitches.
Thread colours
Light tees: black + vermilion red + light grey (tone-on-tone katana). Dark tees: off-white + vermilion red + charcoal (tone-on-tone katana).
Small details
Seal characters are knocked out (shirt shows through). Keep them at least 1 mm stroke. If your digitizer struggles, stitch the seal as a solid red square.
DTF alternative
No embroidery budget for a run? The *-chest-*.png files are textured DTF versions of the same chest art.

    

  


  

    
Next drop: AI art prompts

    
For more complex illustrations, generate the art in Midjourney or Ideogram, then add the kanji yourself with the build kit. The prompts forbid text on purpose.

    
Why no AI text: image models invent fake characters. The two "kanji" in the red stripe of your current samurai tee are not real Japanese, and readers will notice. Every character in this drop is set from a real font and checked for meaning.
